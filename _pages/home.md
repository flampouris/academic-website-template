### Operational Earth Sciences! 

Operational Earth Sciences (OES) systematically and continuously applies Earth science knowledge and tools to monitor, analyze, and predict Earth's systems and processes at relevant time and space scales. This involves constantly collecting, processing, and interpreting data from various sources to provide timely information for decision-making and action.

OES integrates four key elements:
* Continuous observation monitoring: Using networks of sensors, satellites, and other instruments to constantly observe Earth's atmosphere, oceans, land surface, and subsurface.
* Data acquisition, management, and analysis: Gathering and processing vast amounts of data from diverse sources to extract meaningful information.
* Modeling, simulation, and prediction: Using empirical, statistical, numerical, and machine learning-based computer models to simulate Earth's systems and forecast past and future conditions.
* Information dissemination and decision support: Providing actionable information and communicating it accurately and promptly to various stakeholders, including governments, businesses, and the public.

OES has a broad societal impact, from daily weather forecasts to long-term planning for climate resilience, disaster risk reduction, resource management, environmental protection, and economic development.

### About me

As an oceanographer specializing in Operational Earth Sciences, I bring extensive experience in observation, data assimilation, and atmosphere-ocean modeling to the table. My focus is on translating innovative research into practical, operational solutions.

My academic background includes degrees in marine sciences and numerical modeling from the University of the Aegean, followed by an MSc and PhD focused on air-sea interaction from the Universities of Kiel and Hamburg.

My career has been defined by leading scientific development from initial innovation to operational implementation (I2O) at leading organizations. This includes impactful work at the Hereon Institute (Germany), the Naval Research Lab (USA), and within three distinct line offices at NOAA (NWS, NOS, and OAR), as well as contributions to several private companies. I have also held key leadership positions at the Program Office of NOAA/NWS/OSTI, NOAA/EPIC, and in the private sector.

A strong advocate for open science and data sharing, I am dedicated to building robust collaborations and contributing to open-source projects across academia, government, and industry.
